# 2. no I mean it rejects it because an AI has generated that summary?

Ah, I see your concern now.

**Short answer:** No, ATS will **not reject** your resume just because an AI generated the summary.

**Why:**
- ATS scans for **keywords, dates, job titles, and skills** — not for “AI-written” text.
- There is **no AI-detection feature** in standard ATS systems (like Taleo, Workday, Greenhouse, etc.).
- Even if a human recruiter suspects AI wrote it, they won't reject you for that alone as long as the summary is accurate and relevant.

**Caveat:**  
If the AI-generated summary is **vague, generic, or full of buzzwords** (e.g., “passionate, results-driven synergy creator”), that might hurt you — but not because of AI detection. It’s just poor content.

**Bottom line:** Use AI to draft, but **customize it** with your real accomplishments and job-specific keywords.

# 3. what's a cover letter ?

A **cover letter** is a one-page document you submit with your resume that explains why you want the job and why you're a good fit.

**Key points:**
- Addresses the hiring manager directly
- Highlights 1–2 relevant achievements from your resume
- Shows enthusiasm for the specific role and company
- Should be tailored for each job application

**Length:** Typically 3–4 short paragraphs.

**Purpose:** To make a personal connection that your resume alone can't.

# 5. in work experiences is it good to add one or two lines about the companies I have worked for?

**Generally no** — skip company descriptions unless the employer is obscure.

**Why not:**  
- Recruiters know most major companies.  
- Those lines waste space that could hold your achievements.  
- Shifts focus away from *your* impact.

**One exception:**  
Add **one short line** if the company is:
- A small local business
- An industry outsider (e.g., non-tech company applying for a tech role)
- In a different country or niche field

**Example (when needed):**  
> *ABC Logistics – a regional freight brokerage serving the Midwest*

**Bottom line:** Default = don't describe. Use that space for action verbs and results instead.

# 6. should I add a hyperlink to company website in the work experiences?

**No.**

**Why:**
- ATS can't click or read hyperlinks reliably
- Recruiters won't click while scanning quickly
- It adds visual clutter
- Looks unprofessional in a printed/PDF resume

**Better approach:**
- Just write the company name
- If the company is obscure, add a **one-line plain text description** (as mentioned earlier)

**Exception:**  
Only include a link if you're applying for a **digital/design role** and the link is part of a portfolio or GitHub — but put that in a separate "Portfolio" or "Contact" section, not inside work experience.

**Bottom line:** Keep company names as plain text. No hyperlinks.

# 7. should I mention the country and city f company I have worked for or either of them is enough?

# 8. using a hyphen in the middle of the summary which you have used doesn't confuse the ATS?

**No, hyphens do not confuse ATS.**

**Why it's safe:**
- ATS parses hyphens as standard punctuation, not a delimiter or break
- Hyphens are treated the same as spaces, commas, or periods
- They do not trigger a keyword mismatch or rejection

**The only punctuation that can cause issues:**
- Tabs or unusual symbols (★, →, •)
- Fancy quotes (“ ” instead of " ")
- Graphics or text boxes
- Vertical lines or columns

**Hyphen example that works fine:**  
`improved key metrics — helping acquire thousands of new users`

**Better safe alternative:**  
Replace the hyphen with a **period** or **semicolon** if concerned:

> *...improved key metrics. Helped acquire thousands of new users.*

**Bottom line:** Hyphens are fine. Keep them simple (standard dash or hyphen). No ATS will fail because of one.

# 9. is it a good idea to bold keywords in my summary?

**Generally no — do not bold keywords in your summary.**

**Why not:**

| Reason | Explanation |
|--------|-------------|
| **ATS issues** | Some ATS systems misread bolded text as a formatting error or strip formatting entirely |
| **Looks desperate** | To human recruiters, bolding keywords can appear manipulative or try-hard |
| **Breaks visual flow** | Disrupts the natural scanning pattern of a resume |
| **Inconsistent** | If you bold in summary but nowhere else, it looks uneven |

**When it *might* be acceptable:**
- Applying to a **very small startup** (under 20 people) where a human reads every resume directly
- The job description explicitly says "bold your key qualifications" (almost never happens)

**What works better:**
- Use **plain text** with strong action verbs and specific results
- Let the keywords stand out naturally through relevance and proximity to job description terms
- Save **bolding** for section headers only (e.g., **Work Experience**, **Education**)

**Bottom line:** Keep your summary plain text. ATS reads it cleanly. Humans read it smoothly. No bold needed.

| Section | Ideal Number | Absolute Max |
|---------|--------------|---------------|

- Less than 3 in Tools suggests you lack hands-on experience

**Bottom line:** 6–8 for Skills, 4–6 for Tools, 3–5 for Technical. Keep each on one line, comma-separated.

# 20. isn't the pipeline bad for ats which we place btween the company name and date?

**No, the pipe symbol (|) is generally safe for ATS.**

**What ATS handles well:**

| Symbol | Safety | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Pipe ( \| ) | ✅ Safe | Treated as a standard separator, like a space or comma |
| Dash ( — or - ) | ✅ Safe | Also safe |
| Comma ( , ) | ✅ Safe | Most common |
| Space | ✅ Safe | Always safe |

**What ATS struggles with:**

| Symbol | Safety | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Tabs | ⚠️ Risky | Can create columns that misalign |
| Text boxes | ❌ Avoid | Many ATS cannot read inside them |
| Tables | ❌ Avoid | Parsing breaks frequently |
| Fancy bullets (•, →, ★) | ⚠️ Risky | Some ATS misinterpret |
| Multiple columns | ⚠️ Risky | Reading order can break |

**Safe way to write your line:**

> Azki | Tehran, Iran — March 2021 – Present

**Even safer (no symbols at all):**

> Azki, Tehran, Iran, March 2021 – Present

**Bottom line:** The pipe is fine. But if you want zero risk, use commas only. The dash for date range is also fine. No ATS will reject you for a pipe.

You do not need to say "start of January." Just the month is enough.

| Actual | On Resume |
|--------|-----------|
| Started Jan 1, 2023 | `Jan 2023` |
| Ended Jan 1, 2026 | `Jan 2026` |

Recruiters assume +/- a few weeks. Exact days do not matter.

---

### Final Recommendation

**Write this:**

`Jan 2023 – Jan 2026`

**Or with full month names:**

`January 2023 – January 2026`

**Not necessary to write:**

`01/2023 – 01/2026` (fine but less human-friendly)

---

### Bottom Line

| Format | Use? |
|--------|------|
| `Jan 2023 – Jan 2026` | ✅ Best |
| `01/2023 – 01/2026` | ✅ Acceptable |
| `Jan 1, 2023 – Jan 1, 2026` | ❌ Too detailed, unnecessary |

Your format works, but `Jan 2023 – Jan 2026` is cleaner and more professional.
